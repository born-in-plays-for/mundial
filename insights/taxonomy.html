239 Countries. 8 Billion People.

This radial chart shows every one of the 239 countries and territories the app tracks for World Cup 2026, grouped into pie wedges by confederation (UEFA, CAF, AFC, CONCACAF, CONMEBOL, OFC)* sized proportionally to how many teams each governs. A thin ring outside the disc holds the 28 countries that belong to no confederation at all, genuine non-FIFA members, spanning everything from Vatican City to Greenland. Within each wedge, a brown inner zone (scaled so its area matches the true proportion) marks the share of that confederation's teams that qualified for the tournament.

A few observations

CONCACAF: 6 of 35 members qualify by country (17.1%), and 529M of 610 million by population (86.7%). The US + Mexico + Canada (co-hosts) so outweigh the Caribbean islands, Costa Rica, and their likes that the population share rockets far past the country share.

AFC: 9 of 46 members qualify by country (19.6%), and 427M of 4.7 billion by population (9.1%), the lowest population share of the six. It's the most populous confederation by far, yet China, India, Indonesia are sitting this one out.

CAF: 10 of 54 members qualify by country (18.5%), and 472M of 1.5 billion by population (31%). A country-share close to AFC's own, but well above AFC's much lower population-share. What makes CAF's story more striking than Asia's isn't the raw numbers, it's the reason behind them. AFC's giants, China and India, largely sit football out, so their absence from the qualified column is no surprise. Africa doesn't have that excuse: the game is hugely popular and widespread across the continent, so CAF's slim 18.5% country-qualification share, despite that popularity, is the gap worth noting. Another striking point: CAF is a net importer of talent trained in Europe, players who correspond to the diaspora resulting from colonization, born and developed there rather than at home. Bottom line: the human potential is there in Africa, gigantic even. What's still missing is the economic power the old colonial countries have.

UEFA: 16 of 55 members qualify by country (29.1%), and 441M of 875 million by population (50.4%).

CONMEBOL: 6 of 10 members qualify by country (60%), and 339M of 434 million by population (78.1%). Same story as CONCACAF, Brazil alone is nearly half of South America's population, so no surprise its qualified zone tops 75%-ish. But it's actually a different story: Brazil always qualifies (the only country in World Cup history to have never missed a single edition), and 6 of CONMEBOL's 10 members regularly qualify.

OFC: 1 of 11 members qualifies by country (9.1%), and 5M of 19 million by population (28%). So small in population that its wedge barely registers next to Asia's 4.7 billion. Worth noting though: New Zealand's qualification came right after FIFA expanded the tournament to 48 teams, handing Oceania a guaranteed direct slot for the first time; before that it was just a playoff shot. So New Zealand may well become CONMEBOL's Brazil, but for OFC: the one team that's always there.

Non-FIFA countries: 28 of them (Vatican and friends) add up to 8 million people, roughly half of OFC's own 19 million, but practically invisible out of the world's 8.1 billion on the population-wise chart.
