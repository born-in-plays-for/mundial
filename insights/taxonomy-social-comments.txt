Self-comments for the "239 countries / 8 billion people / one World Cup" post

--- Comment 1 (CONCACAF) ---
#CONCACAF (Confederation of North, Central America and Caribbean Association Football) flirts with 100% qualified, population-wise: the US + Mexico + Canada (co-hosts) so outweigh the Caribbean islands, Costa Rica, and their likes that the confederation is ~87% qualified by population... although only 6 countries out of 35 are actually qualified for the confederation.

--- Comment 2 (CONMEBOL) ---
Same story for CONMEBOL: Brazil alone is nearly half of South America's population — no surprise its qualified zone tops 75%. But it's actually a different story: Brazil is the only country in World Cup history to have never missed a single edition, and 6 of CONMEBOL's 10 members regularly qualify, so the gap between the country vs. population charts is not as striking as with CONCACAF.

--- Comment 3 (AFC) ---
AFC (Asia), on the other hand, is the most populous confederation by far (4.7 billion!) yet only ~9% of that population qualifies — China, India, Indonesia are sitting this one out.

--- Comment 4 (OFC) ---
OFC (Oceania Football Confederation) is so small in population — 19 million people across 11 countries — that its wedge barely registers next to Asia's 4.7 billion. Worth noting though: New Zealand's qualification came right after FIFA expanded the tournament to 48 teams, handing Oceania a guaranteed direct slot for the first time — before that it was just a playoff shot. So New Zealand may well become CONMEBOL's Brazil, but for OFC: the one team that's always there.

--- Comment 5 (non-FIFA) ---
Even OFC's 19 million people look enormous next to non-FIFA countries: 28 of them (Vatican and friends) add up to just 8 million people out of 8.1 billion — practically invisible on the population chart.

--- Comment 6 (CAF) ---
Whether you look at it by country (10 qualified out of 54) or by population (472M qualified out of 1.5 billion), CAF is the worst-off confederation at the World Cup.

What's striking is that this has nothing to do with a lack of interest in the sport. Unlike Asia, where giants like China or India largely sit football out, the game is hugely popular and widespread across Africa.

Another striking point: CAF is a net importer of talent trained in Europe, players who almost entirely correspond to the diaspora resulting from colonization, born and developed there rather than at home.

Bottom line: the human potential is there in Africa, gigantic even. What's still missing is the economic power the old colonial countries have to develop it.
